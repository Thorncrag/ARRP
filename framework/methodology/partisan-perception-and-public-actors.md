# Substantive Positions, Partisan Perception, and Public Actors

## Authority and Dependencies

This file is the authoritative detailed rule for candidly disclosing ARRP's substantive positions and their likely partisan perception while maintaining party-neutral standards. The guiding principle and human reversed-control boundary in [`../FRAMEWORK.md`](../FRAMEWORK.md) control. Apply the general language rules in [`neutrality-and-language.md`](neutrality-and-language.md) and the institutional-scope rules in [`scope-and-admission.md`](scope-and-admission.md).

## Load When

Load this file when reader-facing text takes a substantive project position; may reasonably be perceived as aligned with or against a party, movement, administration, or ideology; or prominently introduces a politically salient actor or program, including President Trump or Project 2025.

## Substantive Positions and Partisan Perception

ARRP is independent of political parties and applies party-neutral standards, but it is not positionless. The project may reach and defend substantive judgments about conduct, legal theories, institutional arrangements, and proposed remedies. Applying a rule evenhandedly does not make the resulting position politically neutral in its present implications.

When reader-facing text adopts a substantive project position, or when a reasonable reader could materially perceive the analysis as aligned with or against a current party, movement, administration, or ideological program, disclose enough context to distinguish:

1. established facts;
2. governing law;
3. material legal or factual disputes and uncertainty;
4. ARRP's own institutional analysis or policy position;
5. the position's present political alignment or likely partisan perception; and
6. the party-neutral principle supporting the position and its application if control of the relevant institution were reversed.

The disclosure should be proportionate. The repository front door, About page, topic guide, or other page that owns a recurring position should provide the fuller explanation. A standalone reader-facing page that substantively introduces the position, President Trump as a central case-study subject, Project 2025, or another prominently partisan actor or program should provide a concise first-reference qualifier or a clear link to that explanation. Later references on the same page need not repeat it. Incidental references in citations, case names, source titles, quotations, chronology, navigation labels, metadata, and technical records do not require a qualifier.

Do not describe a position as `nonpartisan` merely because its stated rule would apply to either party. Prefer the more precise claim that ARRP is independent of political parties and that its evidentiary, legal, and remedial standards are party-neutral in application. Where present consequences or likely audience perceptions are politically uneven, acknowledge that fact without allowing it to change the governing standard.

This rule clarifies the existing neutrality, claim-status, political-language, and candor requirements. It does not create a new scoring component, change a component weight, alter an audit threshold, require score rebaselining, or authorize any score change outside an ordinary completed T-audit under the human-approved rubric.

## Public-Actor References

Project-authored prose should use office-respecting, institutionally neutral references for public actors when doing so improves precision and avoids avoidable partisan tone. For example, use `President Trump` rather than `Trump` or `Donald Trump` when referring to him as an officeholder, actor, or case-study subject in the project's own analysis.

When introductory text first presents President Trump as a central case-study subject, briefly explain why the conduct is institutionally relevant and what the project is not treating as the defect. The repository front door uses the canonical concise formulation: `ARRP examines President Trump's conduct because of the institutional weaknesses it revealed or exploited—not merely because the project disagrees with a policy or political position. The same standards apply to any officeholder.` Equivalent wording may be used when the page needs to identify a more specific institutional concern. This qualifier is not required for every later factual reference or for the exact source and technical material excluded by the preceding rule.

This convention does not alter exact source material. Preserve formal case names, article titles, source titles, URLs, direct quotations, formal legal labels, and other citation text exactly as written unless the underlying source itself is being corrected or replaced.

When a phrase such as `Trump-era`, `anti-Trump`, or `pre-Trump` appears in project-authored prose, rewrite it into a more precise institutional formulation, such as `incidents from President Trump's administration`, `framed as targeting President Trump`, or `pre-2017 institutional status quo`, as context requires.

## Project 2025 Treatment

Treat Project 2025 and comparable ideological programs as source material for institutional-risk analysis, not as partisan enemy documents. When Project 2025 is first substantively introduced on a standalone reader-facing page, identify its character and present political association, distinguish its documented contents from ARRP's analysis, and explain that ARRP does not treat every recommendation as an institutional failure or oppose it merely because of its partisan source. The qualifier may be concise and may link to the fuller [Project 2025 topic guide](../../topics/project-2025.md); it need not accompany later references, citations, source titles, navigation labels, metadata, or technical records.

The project may state its opposition to maximalist unitary executive theory, institutional capture, and efforts to subordinate public institutions to personal or factional control. Because those positions currently have politically uneven implications and may reasonably be perceived as aligned against Project 2025 and the MAGA-aligned Republican coalition, reader-facing text should acknowledge that perception where material and then explain the independent institutional basis for the position. Analysis should focus on the mechanism, legal vulnerability, implementation pathway, and institutional effect, and the same standard should apply to a comparable program advanced by any party or officeholder.

Where Project 2025-style unitary executive theory is discussed, distinguish founding-era executive unity from modern maximalist presidential-control claims. The project may characterize the maximalist version as Federalist in pedigree but Anti-Federalist in consequence when the analysis explains that it takes Hamiltonian executive unity and pushes it toward the monarchy-like concentration of power that Anti-Federalists feared. The focus should remain on how unchecked presidential control can hollow out separation of powers, checks and balances, legislative authority, judicial review, or independent statutory administration.

## Collective Labels

Avoid loaded collective labels that imply every Republican, conservative, Trump voter, Democrat, progressive, independent, or other broad political group supports the conduct or institutional weakness being analyzed. Use narrower sourced formulations such as `MAGA-aligned`, `Project 2025-associated`, `President Trump's administration`, `the administration`, `the proposal's sponsors`, or `identified supporters` only when analytically necessary.

Do not characterize an institutional failure as a failure to prevent a political movement from existing or participating in lawful political activity. Identify instead the governmental conduct, officeholders, organizations, or initiatives that exposed or exploited the institutional weakness. Political association alone is not the institutional harm.

## Coalition Reality

The project should not describe itself as a Democratic Party agenda or allow likely audience interest to substitute for institutional analysis. It may, however, acknowledge that certain proposals are likely to be more immediately interesting to Democratic, independent, civil-libertarian, good-government, institutionalist, or other audiences because of the present political alignment of the abuses being studied. That coalition reality should inform adoption analysis, objection handling, and framing, but it should not change the evidentiary standard, legal analysis, remedy design, or neutrality of application.

ARRP is not designed primarily to persuade readers who categorically reject its factual and institutional premises. Relevant cross-partisan audiences nevertheless include independents, former supporters of President Trump or MAGA-aligned movements, politically cross-pressured Republicans and conservatives, institutional conservatives, libertarians, federalists, and other readers willing to evaluate new evidence or reciprocal institutional arguments. Treat receptiveness as a reader posture rather than an attribute automatically assigned by political identity: do not assume that every current supporter is unreachable, and do not divert substantial project space into relitigating the foundational premise for readers who decline to engage it. Framing should allow a receptive reader outside the project's likely coalition to understand the institutional question without requiring the project to soften supported findings or manufacture political symmetry.

## Advocacy Tone

Avoid campaign-style language such as `fight back`, `defeat`, `crush`, `resist`, `anti-Trump`, or `pro-democracy side` in proposal analysis. Prefer institutional verbs such as `repair`, `prevent recurrence`, `constrain`, `restore`, `stabilize`, `harden`, `detect`, `correct`, `deter`, and `contain`.
