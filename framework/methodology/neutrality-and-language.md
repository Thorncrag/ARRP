# Neutral Characterization and Reader-Facing Language

## Authority and Dependencies

This file is the authoritative detailed language standard for project-authored prose. The guiding principle and party-neutral application rule in [`../FRAMEWORK.md`](../FRAMEWORK.md) control. Apply [`scope-and-admission.md`](scope-and-admission.md) when language depends on project scope, [`partisan-perception-and-public-actors.md`](partisan-perception-and-public-actors.md) when a position or actor may reasonably be perceived as partisan, and [`../evidence/annotation-and-source-standards.md`](../evidence/annotation-and-source-standards.md) for claim status and citation support.

## Load When

Load this file whenever project-authored prose is drafted, revised, summarized, or audited; whenever conduct, motive, intent, law, evidence, or uncertainty is characterized; and whenever technical project terminology may appear on a reader-facing surface.

## Neutrality and Language Guidelines

The project applies neutral standards without pretending to be neutral about institutional harm. Language should help a skeptical but fair reader understand the project as structural reform analysis rather than campaign argument, while still naming abuse, corruption, illegality, coercion, falsehood, or institutional damage when the record supports those descriptions.

## Neutral Application

Apply the same evidentiary, legal, and remedial standards regardless of party, ideology, officeholder, administration, or movement. Do not create partisan exemptions, manufacture false equivalence, make unsupported accusations for balance, or design rules that depend on who currently holds power.

## Accuracy Over Softening

Neutrality does not require euphemism. If the record supports a term such as `unlawful`, `false`, `retaliatory`, `corrupt`, `coercive`, `abusive`, or `pretextual`, use the accurate term and cite the supporting basis. If the record is incomplete, contested, pending, or inferential, identify that limitation directly.

## Neutral Characterization

Avoid loaded, pejorative, conclusory, or imprecise shorthand—including a term such as `sham` when it merely substitutes for analysis—in project-authored prose. Describe the conduct, factual predicate, legal deficiency, institutional effect, or supported intent finding instead. Do not automatically replace one loaded label with a more technical-sounding label such as `pretextual`; use that characterization only when the record supports it and the surrounding text explains what makes it accurate.

In proposed legislation, translate a disputed characterization into objective operative criteria wherever possible. Depending on the proposal, those criteria may include the absence of a lawful or good-faith factual predicate, a specified knowledge or intent requirement, an improper case-specific purpose, materiality or causation, and procedural safeguards. This rule does not alter direct quotations, attributed allegations or findings, exact source titles, formal case names, statutory terms, or other source material. Attribute contested characterizations and disclose material limits in the record.

## Motive and Intent

Do not state motive, bad faith, corrupt purpose, retaliation, or pretext as fact unless supported by findings, admissions, contemporaneous records, credible reporting, or a clearly explained documented inference. Use qualified formulations such as `may indicate`, `raises concern`, `alleged`, `reported`, or `not yet adjudicated` when the record requires qualification.

## Conduct Before Character

Avoid standalone character labels and ideological epithets where a conduct-based description would be more precise. Instead of relying on labels such as `authoritarian`, `fascist`, `extremist`, or `radical`, describe the mechanism: concentration of removal power, weakened adjudicatory independence, reduced reviewability, coercive use of funding, manipulation of factual records, or similar institutional effects.

## Reader-Facing and Technical Terminology

Reader-facing project prose should be understandable without prior knowledge of ARRP's methodology. Issue pages, legislation pages, topic guides, area pages, front matter, and public or compiled narrative should not rely on unexplained internal shorthand such as `T0` through `T4`, `Change Audit`, `Internal Remedy-Fit Audit`, `rebaseline`, `fixed zero`, or similar project-specific workflow terms. State the substantive result directly and identify its date when useful. If review provenance matters, use a formulation such as `A July 2026 internal project review found ...` or, when the formal audit character matters, `A July 2026 internal project audit found ...`, with a link to the technical history where appropriate. Do not describe a formal review merely as a `discussion`, and do not use unqualified `audit` in a way that implies independent or external validation.

Exact project terminology remains appropriate in the framework, methodology, audit-history sidecars, dashboards, inventories, internal logs, GitHub Project fields, workflow records, and other technical or administrative surfaces. Machine-readable metadata and links to those records may retain exact tier names. When a reader-facing page must mention a technical term, define it in place or link to a clear explanation so the narrative remains intelligible on its own.
