# Human-Governed Foundation and Development Gates

## Authority and Dependencies

This file is the authoritative detailed rule for the post-admission proposal foundation, delegated development, and maturity gates. The constitutional purpose, human-reserved reversed-control judgment, and governing-authority rules in [`../FRAMEWORK.md`](../FRAMEWORK.md) control. Admission is governed separately by [`../methodology/scope-and-admission.md`](../methodology/scope-and-admission.md). Apply [`../issues/issue-architecture.md`](../issues/issue-architecture.md), [`../REMEDY_FRAMEWORK.md`](../REMEDY_FRAMEWORK.md), and the tier-selection rules in [`../audits/TIERED_AUDITS.md`](../audits/TIERED_AUDITS.md) where implicated. GitHub implementation mechanics belong to [`../GITHUB_WORKFLOW.md`](../GITHUB_WORKFLOW.md).

## Load When

Load this file before substantively developing an admitted issue; determining whether the four-part foundation is present; deciding whether delegated work remains within approved boundaries; making a material change to diagnosis, remedy, vehicle, scope, architecture, or publication posture; or classifying an issue as In development, Developed proposal, Review ready, or Release candidate.

## Human-Governed Foundation and Delegated Development

An admitted issue enters **In development**, and substantive delegated development begins, after four foundations have been established:

1. **Institutional defect:** the independently repairable weakness, its essential boundaries, and its causal relationship to grave arbitrary harm or a material risk of it;
2. **Manifestation or risk pathway:** at least one evidence-backed event, observable condition, near miss, or concrete mechanism showing how public authority operates through the defect and produces—or could predictably produce—the relevant injury;
3. **Remedy:** the selected neutral institutional correction or safeguard and the reason it should reduce the harm or risk without becoming a new arbitrary instrument of power; and
4. **Remedy vehicle:** the selected legal or institutional form, defined sufficiently to guide development but not necessarily already drafted, such as legislation, constitutional amendment, chamber rule, executive action, model state law, or another approved mechanism.

Ordinarily, the human establishes these foundations. The user has also granted foundation-sufficiency authority to **Elim** during authorized recurring runs and to an **interactive Codex agent working directly with the user** during ordinary project work. When the canonical issue record substantively states all four criteria, either may record the determination and move the proposal to `Development level: In development` without waiting for a separate confirmation. This is an evidentiary classification, not authority to invent, choose, or materially revise a missing foundation. A heading, placeholder, tentative list of options, `pending-development` vehicle, or agent recommendation does not satisfy a criterion, but the selected vehicle need not already be drafted. If any criterion is absent, ambiguous, internally inconsistent, or still presented as an unresolved choice, the determining agent must leave the foundation pending, identify the exact deficiency, and skip development that depends on the missing choice.

Candidate investigation is upstream of this four-part proposal-development threshold. An authorized agent may investigate a formal `HOR-###` candidate, verify its premise, build and reconcile its source dossier, apply the canonical Issue-Admission Test, analyze legal adequacy and project overlap, identify possible remedy classes, and prepare a neutral reversed-control analysis and disposition recommendation while the item remains at `Development level: Candidate`. That work may not answer the human reversed-control question, convert the candidate into an admitted issue, select or establish its proposal foundation, create a formal remedy vehicle, score it, or implement a permanent disposition. When the investigation has answered its defined research question as far as available evidence permits, route it for human decision, state the precise recommendation and unresolved uncertainties, and preserve the candidate classification until the human decides.

For an unscored proposal, preserve the determination in canonical issue front matter as `foundation_status: approved`, `foundation_approved_date: YYYY-MM-DD`, and a concise `foundation_approval_note`. The note must identify the human decision or the Elim or interactive-agent sufficiency determination and state where all four criteria appear. `foundation_status: pending` means that one or more foundations remain missing or unresolved. Absence of the field is not permission for any other scheduled agent to infer approval; Elim may evaluate it during its lifecycle-reconciliation work, and an interactive Codex agent may evaluate it while working directly with the user. A proposal already scored through a completed T-audit is treated as a legacy-established foundation unless its current record expressly identifies a foundational question; add the metadata when it is next materially revised.

Once these four foundations exist, an agent is broadly authorized to improve, expand, reorganize, research, draft, test, and otherwise modify the issue and proposal when the work reasonably strengthens the approved diagnosis and remedy. This authority includes adding or improving sources and manifestations; developing legal, implementation, abuse-resistance, fiscal, and adoption analysis; drafting and refining the selected vehicle; repairing internal inconsistencies; responding to audit findings; and making nonmaterial structural, stylistic, citation, and technical corrections. It is not limited to a predetermined checklist.

Human approval remains required to:

- admit, reject, merge, split, retire, remove from active scope, permanently dispose of, or materially rescope an issue;
- define or materially change the institutional failure or its essential boundaries;
- replace the approved remedy or remedy vehicle;
- adopt, reverse, or materially shift a foundational constitutional, statutory, factual, or analytical conclusion;
- materially contract the approved coverage, or expand it beyond the approved institutional failure, remedy, or essential boundaries;
- make a major change to appropriations, staffing, institutional architecture, funding duration, or fiscal responsibility;
- remove or materially diminish important manifestations or evidentiary support when doing so would change the demonstrated pattern or strength of the proposal's justification;
- accept external-review advice that would cause one of these reserved changes;
- answer or revise the human reversed-control question;
- authorize final external circulation or publication; or
- change the project's governing scope, framework, audit rubric, scoring system, or methodology.

These reservations limit decision and implementation authority; they do not remove the subject from the agent's duty of review. Whenever a reserved matter is relevant to the assigned work, the agent must examine the applicable record and evidence, identify the material options and consequences, state its reasoned recommendation and any important uncertainty, formulate the exact decision requiring human authority, and route it for human review. The agent must not omit an applicable issue, audit question, defect, or alternative merely because it cannot make the final choice. It should preserve and continue all nonconflicting work while withholding only the reserved decision and actions that depend upon it.

Every terminal candidate or issue disposition requires a record-specific human decision. Standing, class-wide, or blanket authorization is insufficient. An agent may implement a previously recorded record-specific decision, but it must preserve the candidate or issue, its rationale, and its disposition history rather than deleting the substantive record.

An agent may consolidate duplicative manifestations, remove duplicate sources, replace broken or unsupported citations, update estimates from stronger cited evidence, and shorten prose without separate approval when the action does not materially change substance. The action must remain traceable when it affects evidence, meaning, or a prior project record.

**When in doubt, document the uncertainty, skip the disputed action, and request human review.** In an autonomous or scheduled batch, pause only the affected decision or issue, preserve completed work, state the competing options and the agent's recommendation, and continue with other eligible work. An audit may identify and score a foundational defect, but it must not silently redesign the human-approved foundation.

## Post-Admission Development Gates

Admission establishes only that an issue belongs in ARRP as an independent record. Later maturity follows separate gates:

1. **Foundation established — In development.** Apply the four-part Human-Governed Foundation and Delegated Development rule above. This authorizes development within the established foundation; it does not establish package completeness or quality.
2. **Package complete — Developed proposal.** The initial issue-and-vehicle package performs the [Mandatory Issue Architecture](../issues/issue-architecture.md#mandatory-issue-architecture), makes the Guiding-Principle causal chain visible, and contains enough concrete substance for proposal-quality review. This establishes eligibility for formula scoring but does not itself assign a score.
3. **Audit ready — Audit needed.** The selected T-audit can produce meaningful evaluative findings rather than merely repeat that the proposal or foundation is missing. Read the Project lifecycle fields, canonical page, proposal vehicle, latest audit entry, next step, monitoring predicate, and Change Audit status, then apply the consolidated tier-selection indicators under [`../audits/TIERED_AUDITS.md`](../audits/TIERED_AUDITS.md).
4. **Internally reviewable — Review ready.** A current score of at least 75 establishes readiness for knowledgeable external critique. It does not establish finality, external validation, or publication readiness.
5. **Ready for the human publication decision — Release candidate.** The proposal has a current score of at least 75, a current cumulative T4 with no later unresolved material change, no unresolved publication blocker, and current alignment among governing law and sources, the issue page, proposal vehicle, audit record, Project metadata, and intended publication surfaces. The preferred standard is a total score of at least 79 that includes `External Review Score: 4 / 4`; a score of 79 or higher without those four points does not satisfy the preferred standard. Whenever the preferred standard is not met, the human author must record acceptance of the departure before `Development level: Release candidate` is assigned. Release-candidate classification does not authorize circulation or publication; final authorization remains human-only.

Scores, prior tier completion, word count, citation count, Project development level, and workflow status are useful evidence but are not rigid audit-entry gates. A proposal may be ready to be tested even when it is unlikely to advance, while a superficially complete or highly scored proposal may require development or a Change Audit before another T-audit would be useful.

For recurring autonomous triage, classify each plausible proposal as `Ready for T-audit`, `Development needed first`, `Change Audit needed first`, `Deferred by project decision`, `Blocked by an indispensable prerequisite`, or `Human decision required`. Record monitoring separately wherever an external development is materially relevant but does not prevent work. State the recommended tier and a concise reason. If the scheduled work expressly authorizes tier selection, choose the lowest useful next tier rather than invoking the ordinary clarification rule for an unspecified audit request.
