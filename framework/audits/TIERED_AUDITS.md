# Tiered Audit and Formatting Requirements

## Authority, Loading, and Dependencies

**Authority.** This module is authoritative for the T0–T4 depth system, prerequisites, tier-specific required work and outputs, formatting preflight, current-status review, pending-judicial vulnerability, and tier-scaled budget review.

**Load when.** Load this module before selecting, beginning, advancing, continuing, or closing a T0, T1, T2, T3, or T4 issue-quality audit, and when interpreting whether a prior tier remains current or complete.

**Dependencies.** Load the [Framework kernel](../FRAMEWORK.md), [Audit Core](AUDIT_CORE.md), and [Proposal Quality and Rubric Governance](../scoring/PROPOSAL_QUALITY_AND_RUBRIC.md). Load the verification, legal-review, adoption, and external-review modules at the depth required by the selected tier.

## Formatting Preflight

Every issue-quality audit tier must begin with a brief style, section-fit, concision, and formatting-consistency preflight before substantive scoring or revision. The preflight is meant to catch editorial and template drift, not to become a separate deep audit. It should be scaled to the selected tier and should not materially expand the audit unless it reveals a substantive defect.

**Relocation-before-deletion rule.** Concision should be achieved first by relocating supporting material to **Annotation** and second by consolidating genuine repetition. A formatting, section-fit, or concision review does not authorize removal of a unique factual example, legal proposition, historical analogue, implementation consideration, objection, qualification, source, or unresolved question merely because it does not belong in the main narrative. Misplaced material should ordinarily be moved with the citations supporting it, and affected source-inventory locators should be updated. If proper placement is uncertain, retain the material in **Annotation** and flag the question for later review.

**Limited-removal rule.** Substantive material may be removed only when it is demonstrably duplicative, inaccurate, unsupported after reasonable source review, outside the issue's scope, or superseded by materially equivalent treatment retained elsewhere. Consolidation may remove redundant wording, but every unique proposition and supporting citation should remain somewhere on the page unless one of those grounds applies. Removal of a unique proposition—or any change affecting the issue theory, evidentiary basis, remedy, or scope—must be documented through the applicable Change Audit process.

**Development-stage transition rule.** A candidate or development-stage issue page may temporarily use clearly identified preliminary manifestation hypotheses to organize research. Once concrete examples or evidence-backed observable categories are added—and no later than the first score-bearing audit—the auditor must revisit that scaffolding. Each preliminary category must either be supported and retained as an observable manifestation, consolidated into the cited examples, or relocated to **Institutional Anomaly**, **Underlying Weakness**, **Repair and Prevention**, or **Annotation**. A generic precursor layer should not remain in **Manifestation of the Failure** merely because it was useful before the evidence record existed. Candidate pages that do not yet have evidence-backed manifestations should say so expressly and preserve preliminary categories in **Annotation** rather than present them as established facts.

**Manifestation synthesis test.** For every titled manifestation, the auditor should be able to state in one sentence the distinct factual or observable proposition it establishes. Compare those propositions against one another and against **Institutional Anomaly**, **Underlying Weakness**, **Proposal Survey**, **Repair and Prevention**, and **Annotation**. Subsections that use the same event, authority, statutory history, or evidentiary proposition should be consolidated unless separate treatment materially improves understanding and the distinction is stated. A judicial decision, statutory gap, proposal model, litigation-risk discussion, remedy rationale, implementation concern, or source-development catalog is not by itself a manifestation merely because it is relevant; place it according to its actual function unless it directly establishes the precisely defined institutional failure or documents its observable operation or consequence. Repeated `failure illustrated`, `reform connection`, or similar drafting-workbook labels should be retained only when they add analysis not already apparent from the example or stated in the remedy sections.

At minimum, the preflight should check:

1. the page follows the project's current style and issue architecture, each main section performs its intended function, and any custom main heading materially improves the reader's understanding rather than serving as a container for detail better placed in **Annotation**;
2. each main section is as concise as reasonably possible while still establishing, illustrating, or distinguishing the issue's premise and proposed remedy. Supporting history, extended legal analysis, qualifications, implementation detail, and secondary evidence should generally be placed in **Annotation**. Examples and subsection structure should not be removed merely to achieve concision. **Manifestation of the Failure** may retain titled examples and the factual detail reasonably necessary to support the premise asserted, and factual propositions and real-world examples should retain appropriate nearby citations. Extended chronology, deeper evidentiary treatment, and collateral legal discussion should generally be placed in **Annotation**. Moving material to **Annotation** does not reduce its evidentiary standard: citations should move with the claims they support, and new factual, legal, historical, fiscal, or implementation detail in **Annotation** should receive appropriate nearby citations. These are editorial guidelines, not hard word-count, example-count, subsection-count, or section-length limits;
3. required front-matter fields exist and match the visible issue-page status;
4. the **Issue Snapshot** uses the current blockquote and inline `<br />` format;
5. **Manifestation of the Failure** uses concise, evidence-backed titled examples or observable categories; each subsection establishes a distinct proposition under the Manifestation synthesis test; and legal background, proposal models, source-development administration, remedy tiers, hypothetical design risks, and uncited restatements of the institutional anomaly appear in **Underlying Weakness**, **Proposal Survey**, **Repair and Prevention**, or **Annotation**, as appropriate;
6. **Proposed Legislation**, **Proposed Constitutional Amendment**, or **Proposed Enabling Legislation** uses Markdown bullet links or the approved page-content placeholder `Pending development`;
7. the **Budgetary Impact Statement** has the substantive statement first and the italicized `Note:` disclaimer below it, or an affected JUD-011 issue has two separately labeled pathway statements followed by one disclaimer;
8. **Proposal Scoring** groups all scores and viability indicators at the top, uses the coalition-estimate indentation format when estimates appear, separates audit metadata with an em dash divider, and links the full audit-history sidecar;
9. scoring annotations mirror visible scoring labels where practical, including **Quality Score**, **Adoption Score**, **Coalition Support Estimates**, **External Review Status**, **Adoption Friction**, **Required Electoral Environment**, and **Development Priority** when those fields appear;
10. the issue page, sibling audit-history file, and GitHub Project agree on the fields each surface owns: GitHub should match lifecycle, audit-control, release-triage, and routing fields, while detailed rubric, pathway, development-priority, adoption-friction, legislation-path, and score-basis data should remain on the issue page and audit-history sidecar; and
11. reader-facing narrative uses plain language, describes contested conduct rather than relying on unsupported shorthand such as `sham`, and does not expose unexplained `T0`–`T4`, Change Audit, rebaseline, or other project-workflow terminology. When review provenance is material, prefer a dated `internal project review` or clearly qualified `internal project audit` formulation and link the technical history where useful. Exact terminology may remain in metadata, audit sidecars, framework and methodology documents, dashboards, inventories, logs, GitHub Project fields, and comparable technical records.

Tier scaling:

- **T0:** check the most recent audit entry, visible front matter, Issue Snapshot, Budgetary Impact Statement, Proposal Scoring box, and audit-history link; flag obvious navigation drift only for an existing stable record.
- **T1:** perform the T0 checks and the Navigation Synchronization Check for the repository front door, project-area contents, affected area contents, Subject and Institution Index, GitHub issue registry, and affected local links.
- **T2:** also check GitHub Project field alignment, proposal-to-legislation link shape, annotation scoring labels, and whether prior format findings were carried forward.
- **T3/T4:** also check the audit-history sidecar, source-inventory references affected by visible citations, legislation-page budget/source-note structure, and any template changes adopted since the prior audit.

Formatting preflight corrections may be made during the audit when mechanical. If the preflight reveals a substantive discrepancy between issue analysis, scoring, and proposed legislation, document and report it under the proposal-to-legislation consistency rule rather than silently correcting it without user review.

At audit closeout, repeat the style, section-fit, and concision portion of the preflight against the completed draft. This closing check should confirm that research, examples, legal analysis, opposition analysis, implementation discussion, or other material added during the audit remains in the appropriate reader layer and has not made the main sections unnecessarily long. Preserve useful examples, titled subsections, and nearby citations when tightening the main narrative. Record a material unresolved style, section-placement, or concision concern in the audit output and consider it proportionally in the **Argument and Cogency** assessment; minor or genuinely debatable editorial judgments do not by themselves block advancement.


## Audit Depth Tiers

Audits should be scoped to a stated depth tier before work begins. A smaller tier is not a failed larger audit; it is a deliberately narrower review with its own required checks and outputs. The audit record should identify the tier used, what was completed, what remains unresolved, and what the next higher tier should examine. Tier completion is determined by the required analytical work, not elapsed time, token use, account usage, or another resource budget.

Before starting a new audit for an issue, check the most recent audit record for unresolved blocking findings, skipped prerequisites, source-development tasks, or user-input needs that must be resolved before further audit work can proceed. If blocking unresolved items remain from the last audit, cancel the new audit request, notify the user, and ask whether to resolve the existing items, explicitly override the block, or revise the audit scope. Do not begin a new audit until the user gives direction. Ordinary next-audit recommendations do not block a new audit when the requested audit is meant to address them.

Before starting substantive audit work, confirm whether the issue has a concrete proposal vehicle. Check the issue page's **Proposed Legislation**, **Proposed Constitutional Amendment**, or **Proposed Enabling Legislation** section, any linked legislation files, and the GitHub Project status fields. If the issue has no linked draft vehicle and instead uses the page-content placeholder `Pending development`, another no-draft placeholder, or no proposed-vehicle section, notify the user before proceeding and ask for confirmation. The notice should state plainly that no proposed legislation or equivalent proposal text exists yet, that any audit will be limited to source development, issue admission, remedy selection, or fixed-zero/candidate review unless drafting is added to the scope, and that a formula-based Proposal Quality Score should not be assigned until a concrete draft exists. Do not treat that placeholder as a Project Status or broken link.

If the user requests an audit without specifying a tier, ask which tier to run rather than guessing. The default recommendation should be the lowest level that appears useful, usually **T0: Triage scan** for a new or ambiguous request. Do not silently escalate to a higher tier.

Before running a higher-tier audit, confirm that the immediately lower tier has already been completed for the issue and remains reasonably current. If the lower tier has not been completed, ask the user whether to run the lower tier first or explicitly skip it. If the user confirms skipping a lower tier, record that skipped prerequisite in the audit output.

For an issue fixed at `0` or assigned `current-fixed-status` while `Status: Blocked` because a judicial merits ruling, controlling external finding, completed factual predicate, or comparable indispensable prerequisite is unavailable, the next review should normally be limited to a **blocker-predicate check**. For an issue using `Status: Deferred`, the corresponding review is a **deferral-reconsideration check** at the recorded condition or date. These hold-predicate checks should verify whether the documented prerequisite or reconsideration basis has changed, using current reliable sources appropriate to the issue. Unless it has changed or the user expressly asks for exploratory work consistent with the hold, do not perform substantive proposal development, formula scoring, prior-proposal surveys, budget development, adoption analysis, drafting revisions, or higher-tier publication work. If it remains unchanged, update the issue page, audit-history file, GitHub Project fields, inventories, and sources only as needed to document the check and preserve the fixed-zero status. If it has changed, document the change, identify the next ordinary audit tier, and update Status before resuming substantive work. A pending human-reserved choice uses `Human decision needed`, not `Blocked`.

| Tier | Use when | Purpose | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T0: Triage scan** | The premise is concrete enough to test viability, duplication, mootness, deferral, major reframing, and whether deeper development is warranted. | Catch obvious blockers before deeper work. | One-paragraph status, obvious defects, obvious mootness or material-reframing flags, obvious pending-judicial-opinion flags, and whether deeper audit is needed. |
| **T1: Framework check** | A recognizable issue package exists, including an approved foundation and enough issue, source, remedy, budget, and vehicle material for structural review to be productive. | Check internal project consistency, visible framework compliance, basic current-status risk, and initial enactment-pathway fit. | Short checklist covering structure, links, metadata, legislation link, issue status, remedy type, source-inventory presence, contents-and-subject-index synchronization, registry routing, Budgetary Impact Statement presence and basic classification, obvious unsupported claims, initial Required Electoral Environment, Pathway Viability, Development Priority, Pathway Adjustment, a basic mootness or material-reframing check, and an obvious pending-judicial-opinion check. |
| **T2: Development audit** | The issue and vehicle are substantively developed enough for meaningful legal, evidentiary, remedy, implementation, fiscal, and preliminary scoring review. | Verify the proposal enough to guide revision and provisional scoring. | Component-level score estimate, key source checks, current public-source refresh, mootness or material-reframing assessment, pending-judicial-opinion vulnerability assessment, legal-fit notes, remedy-fit notes, provisional budget or implementation-burden analysis where material, major risks, and next audit needs. |
| **T3: Readiness audit** | Material development-audit deficiencies have been addressed or can now be resolved, and the package is plausibly approaching knowledgeable external circulation. | Test whether a proposal is close to external circulation. | Reproducible score, source-refresh log, verified claims, unresolved claims, mootness or material-reframing assessment, pending-judicial-opinion vulnerability assessment, legal and judicial-scrutiny risks, direct and functional analogue review, source-backed fiscal or burden claims where asserted, adoption/support notes, international score or `N/A`, and recommended revisions. |
| **T4: Publication-ready audit** | The proposal is plausibly close to Review Ready or higher and has no known foundational defect that should plainly return it for human decision or development first. | Resolve every publication concern that can reasonably be resolved through deep public-source research before external circulation. | Publication-readiness memo, cumulative T1-T3 criteria confirmation, final score recommendation, deep source-refresh log, verified and unresolved claims table, mootness and material-reframing determination, pending-judicial-opinion vulnerability assessment, legal-durability analysis, direct and functional prior-proposal comparison, adoption and opposition analysis, source-backed fiscal or burden review including available budget analogues where material, international score or `N/A`, drafting recommendations, and expert-review needs. |

Every audit tier must include a current-status check for mootness and material reframing, scaled to the tier's required analytical depth. The auditor should check whether recent decisions, pending judicial opinions, pending merits cases, certiorari grants, emergency or stay applications, lower-court injunctions, legal opinions, enacted laws, pending or completed legislation, regulations, agency actions, court orders, factual developments, elections, appointments, repeals, superseding events, implementation experience, or other current developments have made the proposal unnecessary, partially obsolete, legally impossible, materially stronger, materially weaker, or structurally different from the issue as drafted. If the check identifies a serious mootness, pending-judicial-vulnerability, or reframing concern, document it prominently in the audit record, update the issue page when source support permits, and adjust the Proposal Quality Score. If reliable current sources show that the standalone proposal has become completely moot, set the score to `0` while that condition remains true.

Every audit tier must also include a **pending judicial vulnerability check**, scaled to the tier's required analytical depth. The check should identify whether the Supreme Court, a federal court of appeals, a relevant district court, a state supreme court, or another controlling tribunal has a pending case, argued case, granted petition, emergency application, stayed order, remand, or imminent merits decision that could materially affect the proposal's constitutional footing, statutory authority, remedy design, enforcement mechanism, standing theory, scope, urgency, or issue-admission result. This includes cases related to unitary executive theory, removal power, appointments, presidential control of agencies, administrative-state limits, spending conditions, election rules, federalism, speech, equal protection, due process, immunity, judicial enforcement, and other doctrines implicated by the issue under audit.

If the pending judicial vulnerability check cannot be completed at the selected tier, mark it `Unresolved` or `Verification pending` and assign no favorable current-status or legal-fit credit for that component. If a pending case is an indispensable prerequisite likely to control the remedy or issue-admission result, the audit should recommend `Blocked`, narrowing, or expressly conditioning the proposal until the decision issues and should state the unblock trigger. If the project instead concludes that work could proceed but should be postponed, recommend `Deferred` with its reason and reconsideration condition. If a pending case merely creates legal risk without preventing work, preserve the ordinary Status, apply `needs: monitoring` when recurring review is warranted, document the risk, and identify drafting adjustments that would remain valid under plausible outcomes.

Audit tiers impose no elapsed-time ceiling, token ceiling, account-usage allowance, or subscription-driven resource budget. An auditor should not truncate a required check, downgrade an audit, or seek renewed permission merely because the work takes longer or uses more resources than expected. Efficiency and avoidance of duplicative research remain good practices, but they do not substitute for completing the selected tier.

The **T4: Publication-ready audit** should be run when the user requests T4, publication-ready review, or an equivalent deep audit. It does not require a separate confirmation step merely because it is T4. Before starting, state the likely scope and note that the audit may still identify issues requiring attorney, legislative-counsel, subject-matter expert, or stakeholder review.

A T4 audit is cumulative. Before undertaking publication-ready analysis, it must confirm that the material criteria from T1, T2, and T3 have been satisfied, carried forward as unresolved, or intentionally scoped into the T4 work. This cumulative check should include framework compliance, formatting preflight, metadata/GitHub Project/inventory alignment, current-status and mootness review, pending-judicial vulnerability, existing-law and prior-proposal review, functional analogues, budgetary-impact treatment, proposal-to-legislation alignment, source verification posture, adoption-pathway posture, and unresolved prior-audit findings. If a lower-tier criterion remains unresolved, the T4 audit should either resolve it, document why it cannot be resolved within T4, or treat it as a publication blocker.

A T4 audit should address every reasonably researchable publication concern, including source verification, current-law status, prior-proposal history, legislative drafting vulnerabilities, constitutional and administrative-law risks, judicial-scrutiny concerns, implementation feasibility, abuse resistance, adoption strategy, opposition arguments, public-support evidence, international implications where material, and consistency with the project's framework and inventories. It should not claim to replace professional legal advice, legislative counsel, empirical polling, fiscal scoring, or stakeholder validation.

Budgetary-impact review is tier-scaled. T1 confirms that the required **Budgetary Impact Statement** exists and uses a permissible preliminary classification. T2 develops provisional funding, workload, or implementation-burden analysis where materially relevant. T3 and T4 source-check any fiscal or burden claims that the issue or proposal affirmatively makes and search for budget or appropriations analogues when the proposal requires funding. Do not include a dollar estimate unless it is tied to a cited government source, historical appropriation, authorization of appropriations, CBO score, agency budget material, audited program cost, or comparable source-backed basis. The substantive classification should appear first, followed by the italicized project disclaimer note. Never describe ARRP's budgetary statement as an official CBO, OMB, agency, or legislative-counsel score.

If a required check cannot be completed because reliable evidence is unavailable, access is unavailable, qualified external review is necessary, or a genuine substantive blocker remains, mark that portion unresolved, assign no favorable credit for it, and identify the exact follow-up need. Lack of time, tokens, subscription capacity, or an internal usage allowance is not by itself a permissible unresolved finding. Do not expand into the required outputs of a higher tier unless the user asks for the deeper audit.

The selected audit tier affects audit scope and confidence, not the weighting of the scoring formula. Do not give a proposal extra credit because it was reviewed under a higher tier, and do not penalize a proposal merely because the user selected a lower tier. Apply the same component weights at every tier. Components not actually reviewed or supported within the selected tier should be marked unresolved and receive no favorable credit until reviewed.

The auditor should maintain balanced coverage across the selected tier's major audit areas. A difficult source dispute, legal question, factual cluster, or research thread should be pursued as far as necessary to satisfy the tier; if it reaches a genuine evidentiary or external-review blocker, document that blocker rather than allowing it to obscure other required checks.

On the first audit run for an issue, complete the selected target tier. Incidental research beyond one component's ordinary depth does not automatically convert the audit into the next tier, and T4 should not be reached by spillover from a lower tier unless the user requested T4, publication-ready review, or an equivalent deep audit.

Future audits should use prior audit results to avoid duplicating still-valid verification and to concentrate on unresolved, changed, or higher-depth components. If prior work showed an area is unusually complex, identify it early and complete or expressly block it on substantive grounds rather than imposing a timebox.

Successive audits should be improvement-targeted. After the first audit, the auditor should identify the unresolved components, penalties, or source gaps most likely to raise the Proposal Quality Score within the selected tier and target work there first. The goal is to improve the proposal's actual quality, reliability, and adoption readiness, not merely to repeat completed checks. A score should rise only when the audit actually resolves findings, verifies support, improves legal fit, strengthens drafting, reduces risk, or otherwise satisfies the scoring rules.
