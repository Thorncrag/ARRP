# External and International Review

## Authority, Loading, and Dependencies

**Authority.** This module governs the reusable method for external-review
status, qualified-reviewer credit, reviewer-scope evidence, conditional
professional-review points, and international-support or relations analysis.
The project scoring profile owns exact status values, point scales, and
formulas.

**Load when.** Load it when external professional review is sought, received,
represented, or scored; when a deeper audit must determine whether qualified
review remains necessary; or when international, diplomatic, comparative,
treaty, alliance, human-rights, or foreign-relations effects are material.

**Dependencies.** Load the [Framework kernel](../../FRAMEWORK.md),
[Audit Core](core.md), [Verification Protocol](verification.md), and
[Proposal Quality and Rubric Governance](scoring.md). Load the project's
scoring profile before assigning exact status or credit, and load legal-review
or adoption methods when the record affects those subjects.

## External Review Status and Qualified Reviewers

Award external-review credit only when the project record identifies the
reviewer category, review date, review scope, relevant domain, material
comments or approval status, and how the review changed or validated the
proposal. Do not identify a reviewer by name, quote private feedback, or imply
endorsement without permission.

Use careful, scope-limited language. Prefer `reviewed by a qualified reviewer`,
`reviewed with comments incorporated`, `reviewed without unresolved material
objection within the reviewer's domain`, or `approved for circulation within
the stated review scope`. Do not call a proposal legally approved,
professionally certified, final, or guaranteed valid unless the reviewer has
authorized that exact characterization in writing.

A qualified reviewer has professional role, training, or institutional
experience relevant to the part of the proposal receiving credit. Depending on
scope, this may include:

1. a licensed attorney who is not reviewing only as a legislator or political
   advocate and has relevant public-law, litigation, compliance, or drafting
   experience;
2. legislative counsel, a professional bill drafter, or legislative attorney;
3. a legislator or legislative staff member with relevant jurisdiction,
   drafting, oversight, or implementation experience, with legal credit
   limited to actual legal expertise;
4. a directly relevant academic or scholar;
5. a government, judicial, administrative, enforcement, ethics, election, or
   comparable practitioner with direct institutional knowledge;
6. a directly relevant civil-rights, civil-liberties, good-government,
   transparency, election, budget, national-security, labor, procurement,
   privacy, or other subject-matter professional; or
7. an affected stakeholder with implementation knowledge, with credit limited
   to feasibility, burden, practical effect, or stakeholder response.

General reader approval, political agreement, media praise, social engagement,
anonymous comments without verifiable credentials, LLM output, expertise
outside the relevant domain, and undocumented review do not qualify by
themselves.

External review affects only its configured component unless the review
actually improves another scored component. A title alone never increases a
score. The audit must identify what changed, what was validated, and what
remains unresolved.

## International Support and Relations Score

When international effects are materially relevant, assess whether:

- comparative democratic, foreign constitutional, or international
  institutional practice supports the reform principle;
- relevant foreign governments, multilateral institutions, treaty bodies,
  democracy organizations, human-rights institutions, or allied policy
  communities support comparable safeguards;
- the proposal affects perceptions of domestic rule of law, democratic
  resilience, anti-corruption, human rights, or institutional stability;
- it affects allied trust, treaty reliability, diplomatic credibility, or
  soft power;
- it creates foreign-policy, national-security, treaty,
  intelligence-sharing, or alliance-management risks;
- public international, comparative-law, diplomatic, or policy sources support
  the reform or identify manageable concerns;
- the proposal anticipates international criticism or misunderstanding without
  distorting the remedy;
- the analysis distinguishes domestic constitutional necessity from
  international preference and does not substitute foreign support for
  domestic legal authority; and
- the assessment is current, sourced, balanced, and includes adverse evidence
  or unresolved uncertainty.

Do not award favorable international credit without current, cited, and
properly characterized evidence. Foreign commentary or government statements
may show salience or perception but do not prove domestic legal adequacy. Use
the project's not-applicable treatment when no material international
dimension exists, and its unresolved or zero-credit treatment when the
dimension may be material but current sources have not been checked.
