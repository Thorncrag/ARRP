# Neutral Characterization and Reader-Facing Language

## Authority and Dependencies

This file is the authoritative reusable language standard for project-authored
prose. The adopting project's guiding principle and party-neutral application
rule control. Apply [`scope-and-admission.md`](scope-and-admission.md) when
language depends on project scope, the project's public-actor conventions when
a position or actor may reasonably be perceived as partisan, and
[`../sources/claims-and-citations.md`](../sources/claims-and-citations.md) for
claim status and citation support.

## Load When

Load this file whenever project-authored prose is drafted, revised, summarized, or audited; whenever conduct, motive, intent, law, evidence, or uncertainty is characterized; and whenever technical project terminology may appear on a reader-facing surface.

## Neutrality and Language Guidelines

The project applies neutral standards without pretending to be neutral about institutional harm. Language should help a skeptical but fair reader understand the project as structural reform analysis rather than campaign argument, while still naming abuse, corruption, illegality, coercion, falsehood, or institutional damage when the record supports those descriptions.

## Neutral Application

Apply the same evidentiary, legal, and remedial standards regardless of party, ideology, officeholder, administration, or movement. Do not create partisan exemptions, manufacture false equivalence, make unsupported accusations for balance, or design rules that depend on who currently holds power.

## Accuracy Over Softening

Neutrality does not require euphemism. If the record supports a term such as `unlawful`, `false`, `retaliatory`, `corrupt`, `coercive`, `abusive`, or `pretextual`, use the accurate term and cite the supporting basis. If the record is incomplete, contested, pending, or inferential, identify that limitation directly.

## Neutral Characterization

Avoid loaded, pejorative, conclusory, or imprecise shorthand—including a term such as `sham` when it merely substitutes for analysis—in project-authored prose. Describe the conduct, factual predicate, legal deficiency, institutional effect, or supported intent finding instead. Do not automatically replace one loaded label with a more technical-sounding label such as `pretextual`; use that characterization only when the record supports it and the surrounding text explains what makes it accurate.

In proposed legislation, translate a disputed characterization into objective operative criteria wherever possible. Depending on the proposal, those criteria may include the absence of a lawful or good-faith factual predicate, a specified knowledge or intent requirement, an improper case-specific purpose, materiality or causation, and procedural safeguards. This rule does not alter direct quotations, attributed allegations or findings, exact source titles, formal case names, statutory terms, or other source material. Attribute contested characterizations and disclose material limits in the record.

## Motive and Intent

Do not state motive, bad faith, corrupt purpose, retaliation, or pretext as fact unless supported by findings, admissions, contemporaneous records, credible reporting, or a clearly explained documented inference. Use qualified formulations such as `may indicate`, `raises concern`, `alleged`, `reported`, or `not yet adjudicated` when the record requires qualification.

## Conduct Before Character

Avoid standalone character labels and ideological epithets where a conduct-based description would be more precise. Instead of relying on labels such as `authoritarian`, `fascist`, `extremist`, or `radical`, describe the mechanism: concentration of removal power, weakened adjudicatory independence, reduced reviewability, coercive use of funding, manipulation of factual records, or similar institutional effects.

## Reader-Facing and Technical Terminology

Reader-facing project prose should be understandable without prior knowledge
of the project's methodology. Issue pages, proposal pages, topic guides,
collection pages, front matter, and public or compiled narrative should not
rely on unexplained internal workflow, audit, scoring, or lifecycle shorthand.
The project profile should identify its exact internal terms. State the
substantive result directly and identify its date when useful. If review
provenance matters, use a formulation such as `A [date] internal project review
found ...` or, when the formal audit character matters, `A [date] internal
project audit found ...`, with a link to the technical history where
appropriate. Do not describe a formal review merely as a `discussion`, and do
not use unqualified `audit` in a way that implies independent or external
validation.

Exact project terminology remains appropriate in the framework, methodology,
audit-history records, dashboards, inventories, internal logs, hosted workflow
fields, workflow records, and other technical or administrative surfaces.
Machine-readable metadata and links to those records may retain exact tier
names. When a reader-facing page must mention a technical term, define it in
place or link to a clear explanation so the narrative remains intelligible on
its own.

## Related Drafting Discipline

Institutional focus and actor selection are governed by
[`scope-and-admission.md`](scope-and-admission.md#institutional-focus). The
adopting project's actor conventions may further specify titles, recurring
actors, and advocacy-tone applications without changing this neutral standard.

## Substantive Positions and Perceived Alignment

An independent project may reach and defend substantive conclusions while
applying party-neutral standards. When a reasonable reader could perceive a
conclusion as aligned with or opposed to a current party, movement,
administration, or ideology, distinguish:

1. established facts;
2. governing law;
3. material disputes and uncertainty;
4. the project's own analysis or policy position;
5. the position's present political alignment or likely perception; and
6. the neutral principle that would apply under reversed institutional control.

Do not call a result nonpartisan merely because the stated rule applies to
either party. State instead that the project is independent and its standards
are neutral in application, while acknowledging uneven present implications
where material.

## Public Actors and Collective Labels

Use office-respecting, institutionally precise references for public actors.
Preserve exact case names, source titles, quotations, URLs, and formal labels.
Replace era or personality shorthand with the specific administration,
conduct, institution, or period meant.

Avoid collective labels that imply every member of a political or ideological
group supports the conduct under review. Name the officeholder, administration,
sponsor, organization, initiative, or sourced subgroup actually relevant.
Political association alone is not an institutional harm.

## Advocacy Tone

Avoid campaign language where an institutional verb is more accurate. Prefer
`repair`, `prevent recurrence`, `constrain`, `restore`, `stabilize`, `harden`,
`detect`, `correct`, `deter`, or `contain` over slogans about defeating a party,
movement, or person.
