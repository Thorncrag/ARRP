# Content Maturity and Development Gates

## Authority and Project Adoption

This file defines the reusable maturity model. The governing project framework,
admission standard, content architecture, remedy standard, and audit method
control their respective subjects.

Each project must adopt a project profile that defines:

- the exact maturity labels and their order;
- the evidence required at each gate;
- who may classify or change each state;
- how maturity relates to the project's work-tracking system;
- any score, review, or publication threshold; and
- the records needed to preserve a classification's basis.

A project profile may add stricter gates or intermediate states. It may not
collapse admission, foundation sufficiency, package completeness, review
readiness, and publication-decision readiness into one undifferentiated state.

## Maturity Is Distinct From Current Work

A maturity classification records the substantive condition an item has
earned. A workflow state records the action, hold, or decision currently
required. Monitoring, audit depth, publication state, and whether someone is
actively working are separate dimensions.

Maturity ordinarily advances only when the record satisfies the adopted gate.
It does not move backward merely because further research, revision, review,
or maintenance begins. Correct an unsupported classification when the
underlying record does not actually satisfy its gate.

## Functional Lifecycle

A project profile should make the following functional boundaries explicit
without treating the reusable standard as a universal list of field values:

- discovery and preliminary intake occur before formal admission;
- a tracked candidate remains subject to the project's admission or
  disposition authority;
- admission establishes that an item belongs in the project, not that its
  diagnosis or solution is complete;
- foundation sufficiency opens the delegated-development boundary;
- package completeness makes substantive evaluation useful;
- review readiness means the package can receive informed critique; and
- publication-decision readiness means the adopted release prerequisites are
  met, not that publication has been authorized.

Projects may use different labels or additional intermediate states if these
authority and evidence boundaries remain clear.

## Intake and Admission

Discovery may arise from a submission, source review, monitoring, research,
litigation, implementation experience, or another project process. Evidence
that belongs to existing work should be routed there. Otherwise, it may support
a preliminary candidate for bounded investigation.

A preliminary record has not yet earned formal admission or a substantive
maturity classification. The designated human authority may decline, combine,
route, defer, promote, or otherwise dispose of it under the project's
preservation rules. Formal tracking does not itself establish admission,
foundation sufficiency, or implementation authority.

## Human-Governed Foundation and Delegated Development

Substantive delegated development begins only after the canonical record
establishes a sufficient foundation. The reusable foundation has four parts:

1. **Problem or defect:** the independently addressable problem, its essential
   boundaries, and the consequence or material risk that makes repair
   warranted;
2. **Manifestation or risk pathway:** at least one evidence-backed event,
   observable condition, near miss, or concrete mechanism showing how the
   problem produces or could predictably produce the relevant consequence;
3. **Remedy:** the selected correction or safeguard and the reason it should
   reduce the consequence or risk without creating a comparable defect; and
4. **Implementation vehicle:** the selected legal, institutional, technical,
   procedural, or other operative form, defined sufficiently to guide
   development even if not yet fully drafted.

The designated human authority ordinarily establishes the foundation. A
project profile may authorize a named agent or role to record that an existing
canonical record already satisfies all four criteria. That is an evidentiary
classification, not authority to invent, choose, or materially revise a
missing foundation. A heading, placeholder, tentative list of options, or
pending vehicle does not satisfy a criterion.

Candidate investigation remains upstream of this threshold. It may verify a
premise, build a source dossier, apply the admission test, analyze legal
adequacy and overlap, identify possible remedy classes, and prepare a neutral
recommendation. Unless a project profile expressly grants narrower authority,
it may not answer a reserved human question, admit the candidate, establish
its foundation, create an operative vehicle, assign a score, or implement a
permanent disposition.

Once all four foundations exist, authorized development may improve, expand,
organize, research, draft, test, and correct the content within the approved
problem definition, remedy, implementation vehicle, scope, and essential
boundaries. The project profile must identify changes that remain reserved to
human authority. Those reservations constrain implementation authority, not
the duty to notice, analyze, document, and route a material question.

## Development Gates

Admission establishes only that an item belongs in the project as an
independent record. A reusable gate sequence then asks:

1. **Is the foundation sufficient?** All four foundation elements are
   substantively present and the required authority has recorded the
   classification.
2. **Is the package complete enough to evaluate?** The canonical content and
   operative vehicle perform the adopted architecture and contain enough
   substance for meaningful review.
3. **Can the selected audit or review produce evaluative findings?** Review
   should not merely repeat that the foundation or package is absent.
4. **Is the work ready for informed external critique?** The project-specific
   quality threshold is met without implying finality or external validation.
5. **Is the work ready for the designated publication decision?** Adopted
   score, review, alignment, and blocker requirements are satisfied.
   Classification never substitutes for publication authorization.

A project profile owns all exact labels, numeric thresholds, required review
depths, alignment surfaces, and classification authority.

## Repeatable Work Around Maturity

Research, drafting, source development, revision, audit, external review,
publication preparation, maintenance, and monitoring are repeatable operations,
not maturity states. They may recur without automatically changing an earned
maturity classification. A material revision should trigger the project's
change-control method; it does not automatically erase prior review or
maturity evidence unless the changed record no longer satisfies the gate.

Finally adjudicated records follow the project's preservation rules. A
deferred or blocked record remains active only while its documented hold is
meaningful. Monitoring remains separate from maturity and current workflow.

## Required Lifecycle Check

Before substantive work, read the canonical content record, any concrete
implementation vehicle, the latest applicable review, the recorded next step,
and the authoritative work-tracking record. Do not change workflow state
merely because a session starts or stops, and do not reduce maturity merely
because further research or revision begins.

At closeout, classify actual maturity, the next action or hold, monitoring, and
publication state separately. Do not award a formula score, review credit, or
run count merely because work occurred; apply the project's exact audit and
scoring authorities.
